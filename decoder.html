How the decoder works

Each marker uses widely published typical adult reference intervals and clinical cut-offs: the American Diabetes Association for glucose and A1c, the National Cholesterol Education Program and ACC/AHA guidance for lipids, the National Kidney Foundation for eGFR, the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements for vitamin D, and MedlinePlus (National Library of Medicine) for blood counts, chemistry and hormones. Where men and women differ, switch the range selector.

Why your report may disagree

Labs set their own reference ranges based on their instruments and populations, so the range printed next to your result always wins. Ranges also differ for children, during pregnancy and for older adults. A value slightly outside a range is common in healthy people — about 5% of healthy results fall outside a typical reference interval by design.

Units

US labs mostly report in conventional units (mg/dL). Many other countries use SI units (mmol/L). Make sure your units match the ones shown before entering a value. For vitamin D, divide nmol/L by 2.5 to get ng/mL.

The decoder is educational and does not diagnose. Review results with a licensed clinician.
