Resources and glossary

The public sources we rely on, and the lab-report words people most often look up.

Trusted sources

  • MedlinePlus Medical Tests
    US National Library of Medicine guides to hundreds of tests.
  • US Preventive Services Task Force
    Evidence-based screening recommendations by age and risk.
  • Centers for Disease Control and Prevention
    Testing guidance for HIV, hepatitis, STIs, diabetes and more.
  • NIH Office of Dietary Supplements
    Fact sheets on vitamin D, B12, iron and other nutrients, including blood levels.
  • CMS: No Surprises Act
    Your right to a Good Faith Estimate if you're uninsured or self-pay.
  • FDA: Home use tests
    What the FDA reviews for at-home tests and how to use them safely.
  • CDC: Clinical Laboratory Improvement Amendments (CLIA)
    The federal quality standards for US clinical labs.
  • National Kidney Foundation
    eGFR, albuminuria and kidney test explanations.
  • American Diabetes Association
    Diagnostic criteria for diabetes and prediabetes.

Glossary

Reference range
The span of results seen in most healthy people, usually the middle 95%. Set by each lab.
Flag (H / L)
A marker that a value is above (H) or below (L) the lab's reference range. Not a diagnosis.
Critical value
A result far enough outside the range that the lab phones the ordering provider immediately.
Fasting
No food or drinks other than water, usually for 8–12 hours before a draw.
Panel
A group of tests run on one sample, such as a CMP or lipid panel.
Requisition
The lab order form that tells the lab which tests to run.
Phlebotomist
The trained professional who draws your blood.
Serum / plasma
The liquid part of blood after cells are removed; serum is from clotted blood, plasma from unclotted.
CLIA-certified
A US lab that meets federal quality standards for testing human samples.
Direct-access testing
Lab tests you order yourself, with a physician order supplied by the provider, without a prior office visit.
Turnaround time
How long until results are reported after your sample reaches the lab.
mg/dL vs mmol/L
Conventional (US) and SI (international) units. Always compare a value with ranges in the same unit.
Good Faith Estimate
A written cost estimate that US providers must give uninsured or self-pay patients on request.
