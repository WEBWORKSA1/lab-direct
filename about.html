About Lab.Direct

An independent guide to direct-access lab testing: what tests measure, what results mean, what they should cost and where to get them.

Why we exist

More people than ever order their own lab tests, whether to save money, avoid a wait or track a condition. The companies that sell those tests explain their own catalogs, and reference sites explain the science, but few places help with the whole decision: which test, how to prepare, what a fair price looks like, where to go and what the numbers mean afterwards. Lab.Direct is built to fill that gap, without selling tests ourselves.

Editorial policy

  • Sources. We build every page from primary public sources: MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine), the CDC, the US Preventive Services Task Force, the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements and guidelines from professional societies such as the American Diabetes Association, the American Heart Association and the National Kidney Foundation. Pages name their sources.
  • Reference ranges. We publish widely used typical adult ranges and cut-offs for education and always tell readers that their own lab's range takes precedence.
  • Review. Health content is checked against its sources before publishing and re-checked when guidance changes. We are recruiting credentialed clinical reviewers (lab scientists, nurses, pharmacists and physicians); reviewer names will appear on pages they review.
  • Prices. Price ranges are estimates compiled from published direct-pay prices at online providers, rounded, and labeled as estimates. They are not quotes.
  • Corrections. If you find an error, tell us. We correct factual errors promptly and note significant changes.
  • No diagnosis. Our tools explain where a value sits relative to typical ranges. They do not diagnose, and they tell readers when to seek care.

How we make money

Lab.Direct is free to use. We earn revenue from display advertising served by Google AdSense, from clearly labeled sponsorships, from lab and provider listings, from referral or affiliate commissions where disclosed, and from reader support. When a link earns us a commission, it's marked as sponsored. Advertisers and partners have no say over what a test page says.
